# Orphans

A place for names, fragments, and inspiration that aren't (yet)
canonical in `content/`. Things lifted from older worldbuilding
projects, things proposed and set aside, things that might still
be useful but aren't ready to load-bear.

Anything in this file is **not** canon. Pulling something into
`content/` is a deliberate move, not an automatic one. Most of
what's here will probably stay here.

## From the original Bal Rochan article (5 years old)

Source: a self-contained article describing a city-state called
Bal Rochan in a world called Panagua. Panagua was an earlier name
for what is now Bayurinda. Bal Rochan is being used as the seed
for the **deep settlement** on Bayurinda's seafloor, but most of
the article's vocabulary and structure does not survive
translation into the compendium. What's listed here is what was
explicitly _set aside_ during that import. See the deep entry on
Bayurinda (when written) for what _did_ carry through.

### Names and people

- **Sylte Montaire** — the original article's ruler ("Premiere"),
  a pragmatic head of state who excels at diplomatic
  relationships. The _role_ persists as a candidate for the head
  of the largest deep settlement; the _name_ is retired. _Sylte_
  is a French-leaning name that doesn't fit a sea-floor people
  who speak their own tongue (not Bayurindan, not French). When
  this character is brought back in, she needs a name from the
  deep's own language. Sylte stays here as a tonal note: cool,
  short, clean syllables, the kind of name a diplomat carries.

- **House Montaire**, and the **seven major / eighteen minor
  Houses** structure generally — dropped. The function of
  bundled kin-and-craft corporations may still be real on the
  seafloor, but the capital-H "House" vocabulary imports too
  much European-fantasy furniture for a deep-sea polity. If the
  social form comes back, it comes back under a different name
  and probably a different shape.

### Political vocabulary

- **Premiere** — French-republican titling, retired.
- **Citizens / citizain** as a defined voting class — retired
  along with the Houses.
- **The Republic of Bal Rochan** as a political form — retired.
  Whatever the deep call their polity, it isn't "the Republic."

### Class system

The article had a numbered four-rung class ladder (priests / war
heroes / mentors at the top; workers at the bottom). The ladder
itself is dropped — too cleanly Platonic, and the article's own
top rank already wobbles (three different kinds of person stacked
together). What survives, possibly, is the **marked / unmarked**
distinction: those who have undergone the dangerous rite of
passage and those who have not. That is a real social shape; the
numbered tiers are not.

### Aetheriorator

The article's name for the surgical / biological modification
that marks the upper classes. The _thing_ — a dangerous,
sometimes-lethal modification granted by priests, distinguishing
those who carry it from those who don't — is interesting and may
survive. The _word_ is retired (reads as invented sci-fi, not as
in-world Bayurindan or deep speech).

### The blood war

A near-extinction war in the article's deep past, fought over
control of "genetic resources." The _event_ survives as a
candidate dark-past for the deep — a half-remembered catastrophe
that the priesthood's lineage-stewardship work is partly there to
prevent recurring. The _name_ "blood war" is retired (too
generic; not in-world voice). What it should be called in either
Bayurindan or the deep's tongue is open.

### Tonal notes — things to preserve when this material lands

- The hero who dies for a higher ideal as the dominant story-shape:
  _"In the end the hero always dies, sacrificing themselves for
  some higher ideal."_ That is the kind of self-mythology the
  deep would carry, and it's worth preserving if and when this
  material lands as canon.
- The navy as the cross-factional formation, under the head of
  state alone, with naval service as expected of all leaders.
  Survives almost intact in shape, though the institutional name
  needs translation.
- The boulder home: a massive concave lava-stone boulder held by
  coral pillars, domed with bone and seaweed grown into coral.
  Lava-stone _from the old world's core_, predating any
  surface-civilisation ruins. This is the physical seed of the
  deep settlement and should carry through almost as written.
