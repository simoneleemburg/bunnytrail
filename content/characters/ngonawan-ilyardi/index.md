Ngonawan Ilyardi is the serving Oracle of Interpretation within the
[[social/oracle-triad|Oracle Triad]] of the
[[social/sisters-of-bellona|Sisters of Bellona]] — called, in the
order's older formal language, the Sacred Serpent of Resonance.

He belongs to a rare serpent-humanoid lineage native to the
liminal, water-adjacent civilisations of [[concepts/asthera|Asthera]]:
a people whose cultures grew along the edges where land gives way
to estuary, brackish marsh, and slow tidal river. The lineage is
small and scattered; Ngonawan is one of the few who has carried it
into formal service outside its home regions.

## In the Triad

As Oracle of Interpretation, Ngonawan does the work of turning
resonance into meaning. Where another practitioner of
[[concepts/nearing|Nearing]] might come back from contact with
[[concepts/nareth|Nareth]] holding sensation, image, dread, or
unspeakable certainty, Ngonawan finds the structure inside it — the
signal beneath the noise — and renders it into language that the
order, and the wider world, can act on.

It is a function that requires both fluency with the unstable and
the discipline to hold it still long enough to read. Ngonawan's
nature suits the work. His lineage's long-standing intimacy with
threshold places — neither land nor water — gives him a habit of
attention that does not insist things be only one thing at a time.

## Identity and transformation

Ngonawan is a trans man, and his sense of self is bound up tightly
with what transformation means and how it is honoured. In a
cosmology where form is mutable on one side of the veil and
persistent on the other, his own lived transition — the slow,
deliberate work of bringing identity into a body that holds it — is
not incidental biography. It is, in a small but real sense, a
practice cognate with the work of the Triad: the negotiation of
form and resonance, the patient making of a self that fits.
