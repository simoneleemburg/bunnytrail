The space between the worlds is not empty.

It is not, in the usual sense, a place. There is no surface to
stand on, no atmosphere to breathe, no fixed geometry to map.
But it is full — full of what falls into it and what was already
there before anyone had a name for any of it. Pilots passing
through call it the Void, the storm-field, the breaking-place,
the long fall. The
[[culture/knights-of-bellona|Knights of Bellona]] use a more
neutral term in their charts: _the Void Between Worlds_. The
neutrality is doctrinal. What the Void actually is, the order
has not committed itself in public to saying.

## What is in it

Several distinct things are in the Void, and the boundaries
between them are not clean.

**Broken [[beings/naya|Naya]].** Naya who could not hold
identity in some encounter — through failed
[[cosmology/binding|binding]], through unsupported
[[cosmology/nearing|Nearing]], through whatever it is that
breaks Naya — sometimes do not return to
[[cosmology/nareth|Nareth]]. They dissipate instead into the
medium between worlds, where their resonance lingers without
ever quite resolving into anything coherent. A pilot who
passes through a region of broken Naya feels them: drifts of
intent without a self behind it, edges of feeling that arrive
without a source.

**Fragments of the old breaking.** The Void carries fragments
of the same shattering that
[[cosmology/skyblood|Skyblood]] derives from — the breaking that
by long tradition is called a shattered god, though that
attribution varies between traditions and is not settled in
this compendium. Whatever it was that broke, the breaking is
older than the order, older than the worlds' current arrangement
of themselves, older than most of what is written down. These
fragments are not Skyblood. Skyblood is what has been refined
out of the breaking. What remains in the Void is the unrefined
matter, and it does not stabilise identity the way Skyblood
does. It scatters it.

**Failed Eidolons.** The Eidolons of pilots who could not
maintain coherence in transit — who dissolved, overpowered,
missed a catch, or encountered something they could not
survive — drift in the Void with their Naya still partially
present, still partially the construct they were. Some of these
wrecks are recognisable. Some are not. Some appear to still be
piloted, in a sense the language of the order has never quite
settled on.

## The storm-field

These three things together make the Void what it is. It is a
field of unresolved resonance, broken substrates, and the
half-presences of identities that did not finish coming apart.
Pilots speak of it as weather — squalls, currents, fronts —
and the weather metaphor is not metaphor. The Void's storms
are real, and they are storms of identity rather than of air.

To be caught in a Void storm is to be pressed against the same
forces that broke whatever is broken there. A coherent pilot
in working contact with their Naya can pass through. An
incoherent one becomes part of it.

This is why the [[cosmology/world-pillars|World Pillars]]
launch with such violence and catch with such precision. The
Void is the cost of getting it wrong. The order's charts mark
the worst regions and the practice teaches pilots to read for
others. Both kinds of marking are incomplete.

## The cosmic graveyard

The Void is, in the end, a graveyard of failed identity
cohesion. Every Naya that did not hold, every Knight who did
not make it back, every wreck of an Eidolon whose Skyblood
core still hums under the storm-field — they are all in there
somewhere. Some traditions on Bayurinda treat the Void as a
kind of long memory of the cosmos: a record, in resonance, of
every selfhood that did not stay itself.

That reading is contemplative rather than doctrinal, and the
order does not endorse it. The order's own position is that
the Void is dangerous, that it should be travelled through and
not toward, and that what is in it is best left where it lies.
