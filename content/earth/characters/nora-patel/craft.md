## Frame

- **Age**: 29
- **Personality type**: Type 6 — Loyalist
- **Top three values**: belonging, stability, integrity / authenticity

## Inner architecture

- **Desire**: to belong and be valued.
- **Fear**: feeling unworthy and invisible.
- **Misbelief**: _"I have to prove my worth to others to deserve
  love and happiness."_

### Where the misbelief came from

She was raised by her single mother — a woman who often doubted
herself, who could be paranoid at times, and who hoped, more or
less openly, that Nora would find a good man so she could be
happy. The shape of love Nora absorbed from that household is
that love is something you secure, and worth is something you
demonstrate.

### What she most criticises others for

Failing to show empathy, or to be reliable.

## Skills

- Software engineering — her profession, and the lens through
  which she analyses most other things.
- Analytical thinking. She is the one who has actually read the
  thing.
- Quiet resilience — not a flashy or visible strength, but
  durable, and one of the things she most relies on in herself.

## Love language

**Words of affirmation.** She deeply values being verbally
reassured of her worth and importance. Genuine compliments,
expressions of gratitude, and affirming words help her feel
secure and appreciated.

## How she responds to emotional pain

- **Self-reflection and overthinking.** Turns inward. Replays
  events. Looks for the place she went wrong and assumes she
  will find it; sometimes finds one whether or not it was hers.
- **Seeks validation.** Reaches for trusted people or systems
  for reassurance. Struggles when the people she leans on don't
  meet her expectations there.
- **Determination to fix things.** Eventually faces the pain
  head-on. Seeks closure, or confronts whoever caused it, or
  channels the energy into making things right in a way that
  reaffirms her worth to herself.

## Object she can't part with

A delicate silver bracelet from her mother. Simple, possibly
with a charm or an engraved inscription. Understated but
meaningful. She wears it daily.

## Daily outfit

Top to bottom: a tailored blouse or sleek turtleneck in neutral
or soft tones — white, muted blue. Slim-fit trousers in darker
shades, black or navy. On cooler days, a lightweight structured
jacket with subtle metallic accents that hint at her technical
profession. Flats or low-heeled boots, sleek and seamless. Small
stud earrings. The bracelet. A compact crossbody bag or satchel,
always prepared with essentials. Polished, composed, capable.

## How she manipulates

When she does — and she does — these are the registers she
reaches for:

- **Emotional appeal**: _"I thought you'd understand why this
  matters to me."_
- **Strategic self-doubt**: _"I don't know if I can handle this
  alone."_
- **Rational justifications**: _"This is the only way we can make
  sure things work out for everyone."_
- **Guilt-based pressure**: _"I can't believe you'd leave me to
  handle this after everything we've been through."_

## Voice — feminine rage / vulnerability

The register she breaks into when she is, finally, not guarding
herself:

> "You don't want anything from me. That's what feels strange.
> I don't have to guard myself around you. Not once."

> "You don't flinch when I get sharp. You don't back away like
> I'm scaring you. I didn't realize how much I needed that."
