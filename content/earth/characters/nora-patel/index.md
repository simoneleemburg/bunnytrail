Nora Patel is a software engineer, twenty-nine years old, South
Asian, raised in a single-mother household. She carries herself
with a kind of composed hopefulness, which most people read first
and the rest of her later.

## Bearing

Brown skin, straight black hair past her shoulders, dark eyes
that arrive on you with more weight than her manner suggests.
Medium build. The first impression is approachable; the second,
slightly later, is that she is paying close attention.

She dresses for the work: tailored blouses or sleek turtlenecks
in white or muted blue, slim trousers in black or navy, a
structured jacket on cool days with the small metallic accents
that the work-aesthetic of her profession favours. Flats or
low-heeled boots. Stud earrings. A compact crossbody bag with
the day's essentials in it.

## The bracelet

The one piece of her appearance that is not chosen for the work
is a thin silver bracelet from her mother, simple, with an
engraving she does not show. She does not take it off. People
who have known her a while learn not to ask about it directly.

## Manner

Reliable in the kind of way that becomes invisible — work done,
on time, well — and so somewhat under-credited. She is a careful
listener, an analytical thinker, the colleague who has read the
thing closely enough to say what is actually wrong with it.
Quietly resilient.

She values, in roughly this order, belonging, stability, and
keeping faith with what she takes herself to be. She is hardest
on people who fail at empathy or at being depended on.

## What she watches for

She has, by her own admission, a tendency to turn things over in
her mind for longer than the things deserve, and to assume the
fault is hers before the evidence is in. Words said directly to
her — the simple, affirming kind — land harder than she would
prefer to admit. She is working on letting them.

## Heard

> don't have to guard myself around you. Not once."

> "You don't flinch when I get sharp. You don't back away like
> I'm scaring you. I didn't realize how much I needed that."
