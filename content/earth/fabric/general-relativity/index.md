On Earth, the standing account of how distance and time fit
together is **general relativity** — Albert Einstein's 1915 theory
of gravity, in which what feels like an attractive force between
masses is instead the geometry of spacetime itself, curved by the
presence of mass and energy. Objects in free fall follow the
straightest possible paths through that curvature; what Earth's
older physics described as a force, the new picture describes as
a shape.

A few of its consequences matter here:

- **The speed of light is the universal speed limit on causal
  influence.** No signal, no piece of matter, no information of
  any kind travels faster than light through a vacuum. This is not
  a limit on Earth's instruments; it is a limit on the universe.
- **Simultaneity is relative.** Two events separated in space do
  not have a single objective "at the same time." Different
  observers moving differently will, correctly, disagree about
  which happened first. There is no universe-wide present moment
  that all observers must share.
- **Time passes differently in different gravitational potentials
  and at different speeds.** Clocks deep in a gravitational well
  tick slower than clocks far from any mass; clocks moving fast
  relative to an observer tick slower than the observer's own.
  These effects are not illusions; Earth's satellite-navigation
  systems would fail within minutes if they were not corrected
  for.

General relativity has been tested for over a century — light
bending around the sun, the precession of Mercury, gravitational
waves from merging black holes, the existence of black holes
themselves — and has passed every test, often to many decimal
places. Within the regimes Earth can probe, it is as well
established as any scientific theory ever has been.

It is also known to be incomplete. It does not reconcile with
quantum mechanics, the other pillar of Earth's modern physics,
and it breaks down at the singularities its own equations predict
(the centres of black holes; the first moments of the universe).
Whatever the full picture is, general relativity is not it —
only an extremely accurate approximation in the regimes Earth has
been able to look at.

That last caveat is the one that travels. On Earth's account,
there cannot be a universe-wide shared "now" relating distant
events — and Earth does not yet know what else the universe is
doing that general relativity does not describe. See
[[foundation/fabric/space-time-and-identity-shift]] for how this
sits alongside Aurethia's record, which agrees on the absence of
a shared moment and adds a further layer Earth has not yet
chronicled.
