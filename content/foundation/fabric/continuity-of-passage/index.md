A thing in [[mundus|Mundus]] moves along a continuous path in
ordinary space. Whatever else changes about the journey — its
[[subjective-time|subjective duration]], the texture of what is
encountered, the way the surrounding terrain answers the
traveller's inner state — the path itself, traced in the space
all things share, is continuous. There are no gaps. Nothing
arrives somewhere without having passed through everywhere in
between.

This is the spatial counterpart to the duration law. Subjective
time governs the **rate** at which a journey accumulates;
continuity of passage governs whether a journey can be **skipped**.
It cannot. Whatever the traveller does in εσδ-space — rotate
their frame, lean toward a Cardinal, soften their attachment to
ordinary space — they must still cross every metre of physical
distance their path includes.

## What the law says

Between any two points in ordinary space, the path a thing takes
from one to the other is continuous. The path may be long or
short, straight or winding, fast or slow in any clock. What is
encountered along it may be exotic — strange terrain, leaned
landscape, the medium between worlds. None of that lets the
traveller omit a section. To go from one room to another, the
body crosses the threshold. To go from one world to another, the
body crosses the space between them.

A traveller locked in a sealed room cannot near themselves out of
it. They may lean toward any Cardinal they like; they may
[[foundation/fabric/nearing|near]] until their subjective duration
runs long enough to fill the time the rescuers take to arrive.
The walls remain. They are still in the room. To leave, they must
go through a wall, a door, or a window — by whatever means
ordinary or extraordinary, but along a continuous path.

## What the law does not say

It does not say the path is short. A traveller crossing a
rotated region may walk what feels like a mountain range to
cross what an outside observer measures as a meadow. The path is
continuous in both descriptions; its length, by either measure,
is a separate question.

It does not say the path is straight. A traveller deep in a
Self-rotated region may find that the way out winds through
landscape no map would predict. The shape of the path can answer
to the lean. Its continuity cannot.

It does not say which path is shortest, or that observers in
different frames would agree on the answer. "Shortest" is always
shortest **against some metric**, and displacement is what
changes the metric. A path that is a geodesic in the Equilibrium
frame need not be a geodesic in a Self-rotated frame; a route
that is the natural one from inside a lean may look roundabout
from the outside, and vice versa. Neither frame is wrong; each
has its own geometry and its own shortest paths through it. The
law fixes only that **some** continuous path exists and is the
one the body traverses. Which path that is, and what it would
be called by which measure, the law does not fix.

It does not say what is encountered along the path. A leaned
region contains the local material expressing itself under the
lean; an unleaned region contains ordinary ground. A nearing
practitioner may meet things on the way that a passive traveller
would not. The contents of the path are not constrained by
this law. Only its existence as a continuous path is.

## What is ruled out

Teleportation is ruled out. No practice, no lean, no displacement
permits a thing to be at one ordinary-space location and then at
another without the intervening passage. The εσδ-frame is not a
shortcut around physical space; it is a description of how
physical space is **expressed** to a thing displaced within it.

Folded distance is ruled out in any literal sense. Two ordinary-space
locations remain at the distance they are at. What [[nearing|nearing
distance]] does — and what a Knight piloting an
[[kinds/eidolon|Eidolon]] does at cross-world scale — is loosen
the traveller's relation to that distance, change the rate at
which they cross it, and change what crossing it feels like.
The metres are still there. The Knight still passes through
every one of them.

## Why the law holds

Mundus is one ordinary space. The εσδ-frame is not a second
geometry layered on top; it is a description of how things sit
within Mundus relative to its three [[kinds/cardinal|Cardinal]]
limits and its [[kinds/horizon|Horizons]]. A rotated frame does
not move a thing to a different ordinary-space location; it
changes what the thing **is, locally, in the space it already
occupies**. To occupy a different ordinary-space location, the
thing must move through ordinary space to get there.

The asymmetry between the two laws is exact. Displacement in
εσδ-space dilates subjective duration without bound, because the
basin is asymptotic and the work of being displaced has no
ceiling. Displacement in εσδ-space does **not** dilate spatial
extent: the space is what it is, and the lean is over the space,
not a deformation of it. Time can run longer; distance cannot
run shorter.

## Corollaries

Crossing between [[clusters|clusters]] is bounded by this law.
An Eidolon Knight Nearing between worlds still traverses every
unit of physical distance between origin and destination. The
two stacked Nearings — the piloting Nearing and the
distance-softening Nearing — change the rate of crossing and the
character of the medium being crossed. They do not contract the
medium itself. The
[[aurethia/fabric/eidolon-starfaring|starfaring entry]] is
explicit about this: raw distance does not go away; the pilot
does not teleport; the space between worlds remains vast.

Traversing a [[mundus-frame|rotated region]] is bounded by this
law. The feudal-Japan mountain a traveller meets in
[[aurethia/places/realms/valdor|Valdor]] is local material under
the Self-lean, and the traveller crossing it is taking continuous
steps in ordinary space. Walking out of the rotated region is
walking; the path is the path.

The dissolution of a [[kinds/naya|Naya]] back toward
[[aurethia/places/realms/nareth|Nareth]] is not a counterexample.
While the Naya is a discrete thing in Mundus, its motion is
continuous. The return to Nareth is dissolution from a
continuous location, not relocation of a discrete thing across a
gap. Nareth itself is an εσδ-orientation, not an ordinary-space
destination.

Nareth's whispers are not a counterexample either. A whisper
reaching a practitioner is not the practitioner travelling and
is not Nareth travelling. It is resonance carried through the
local material's lean. Nothing crosses ordinary space gaplessly,
because nothing crosses ordinary space at all in this case.

Non-generic geometry is not a counterexample. A singularity or
exotic connection — a region where εσδ-curvature is extreme
enough that the ambient metric stops describing the local
behaviour — still provides a **continuous** path. A body
using such a connection traverses every point along it. The
connection may link locations the ambient geometry would call
far apart; the law does not guarantee that "far apart" is
metric-invariant across all geometric features. It guarantees
that whatever the path is, it is continuous, and that the body
walks it.

## What this is not

The law is not a claim about the path's **measurability** in
ordinary terms. A path through deep displacement may be hard to
describe in metres and minutes; the law does not require the
description to be easy. It requires only that the path exist and
be continuous.

It is not a claim about predictability. What a traveller will
meet on the path, where the path will lead, how long it will
feel, all depend on the displacement and the lean. The law fixes
the topology of motion, not its character.

It is not a moral or narrative principle dressed as physics.
Things in Alteria do not arrive without travel because the
geometry does not permit it. The closed door is not a metaphor;
it is a wall.

What this law shares with the other two — the commitments that
hold no matter which frame is doing the looking — is set out in
[[what-the-laws-preserve|what the laws preserve]].
