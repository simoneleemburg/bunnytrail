Mundus is where existence happens.

It is the bounded interior of reality — the region in which
things have mass, hold form, persist across time, occupy space.
Everything that is, is in Mundus. Bodies, planets, the
cosmos as a material arrangement; selves, civilisations, the
remembered shape of their continuities. All of it is here.

## Bounded by horizons

Mundus is bounded, but not walled. Three horizons mark its
limits: [[existence/source|the Source]], [[existence/self|the
Self]], and [[existence/chaos|Chaos]]. They are asymptotes to
mass — directions massive things can lean toward but cannot
reach. A thing in Mundus is always at some distance from each
horizon, leaning toward one or another by however much its
nature permits, without ever leaving Mundus by arriving at one.

This is the geometry: Mundus is the interior, the horizons are
its limits, and nothing with mass is anywhere else.

## What is and isn't claimed at the foundation layer

Mundus has no centre and no edge. Different regions of it lean
toward different horizons; some regions lean toward none in
particular. None of these regions are different _realms_ — they
are all Mundus, differing only in which horizon they lean
toward and how hard.

Whether anything exists _at_ the horizons — anything without
mass, native to a limit — is left open. Such an entity, if any,
would not be in Mundus.

## Local accounts

Aurethia knows the broad central region of Mundus —
particularly the stable, embodied, persisting parts of it — as
[[realms/asthera|Asthera]]. The Aurethian framing emphasises
Asthera as the layer of stable form and continuity, paired with
[[realms/nareth|Nareth]] (the region near the Source) as its
counterpart. The foundation layer registers Asthera and Nareth
as both being parts of Mundus, leaning differently.

Other clusters' accounts of Mundus are most of what they call
their physics, their geography, their natural history. The
foundation layer does not enumerate them.
