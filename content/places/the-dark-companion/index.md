The Dark Companion is the invisible second body of a
neighbouring binary star system, visible from
[[cosmology/realms/asthera|Asthera]] only by what it does to its
companion star. A single star is observed; that star orbits
something the eye cannot resolve. The thing it orbits is the
Dark Companion.

The compendium does not, in its own voice, say what the Dark
Companion is. The cosmology has not settled the question, and
the entry does not pretend otherwise. What it can record is
what is known: the orbital fact, the cultural readings, and
the more difficult fact that something about it bends the
practice of [[cosmology/nearing|Nearing]] when one travels
within reach of it.

## What is observed

Across all three Sister Worlds, careful observers — astronomers
on [[places/nebelheim|Nebelheim]], contemplatives on
[[places/bayurinda|Bayurinda]], pilots and pilgrims everywhere
— have known for a long time that a particular nearby star
behaves wrongly. It does not move as a single star moves. It
orbits something. The something gives off no visible light, and
none of the technical means available to the Sister Worlds —
which are not advanced telescopically — have ever resolved it.

The system is, in scholarly use, the _Hollow Binary_. The
visible companion has its own name in each tradition. The
Dark Companion has many.

## What it is called

The principal accepted readings, by the worlds that name it
most often:

- _The Dark Companion_ — the most widely used scholarly term.
  Neutral, descriptive, the name the order's charts default
  to.
- _The Hollow Companion_ — the older Bayurindan scholarly
  reading. Preferred in the contemplative traditions where the
  emphasis falls on what is _absent_ rather than on what is
  _dark_.
- _The Mouth of Returning_ — a Bayurindan pilgrim reading.
  Used in the traditions that hold that what falls into it
  comes back changed, and that the changing is the point.
- _The Ash-Eye_ — the Nebelheimic reading. A divine wound; a
  hole left by something that departed. Used in the world's
  older liturgical material more often than in its working
  language.
- _The Pilgrim Star_ — the Sharazani reading. Named for what
  happens to travellers who go near it. Used in performance
  traditions and in the more elaborate written histories.
- _The Unholding_ — an oracle reading. Used by those whose
  work is interpretation of resonance phenomena, where the
  Dark Companion is named for what it does rather than for
  what it is.

The readings agree on direction without agreeing on definition.
What is common to them is _absence that acts_ — a place where
something is missing in a way that affects what happens near
it.

## What it does to Nearing

The compendium is on firmer ground here. Pilots who travel
within reach of the Dark Companion report consistent effects.

[[cosmology/nearing|Nearing]] distorts near it. The familiar
loosening becomes less controlled — softer when the pilot
intended firmness, harder to interrupt when the pilot intended
release. The currents of
[[cosmology/interplanetary-travel|interplanetary travel]] bend
toward the Hollow Binary in ways the order's charts attempt to
mark but cannot fully predict.

The longer a pilot remains in its vicinity, the more difficult
the work of holding a stable self becomes. Travellers describe
the feeling as _falling further than the journey accounts
for_, or as _hearing the wrong echoes back_, or as the sense
that something is reading them across the distance. Whether
any of these descriptions is literally accurate is unsettled.
The reports are consistent enough that the order treats them
as a working hazard rather than as imagination.

What is recorded, then, is this: the Dark Companion makes
Nearing harder, and the harder Nearing fails in directions
that are not always the failures Nearing usually fails in.

## What the worlds say about it

The Sister Worlds do not agree on what the Dark Companion is.
The compendium records the readings without choosing among
them.

- On **Nebelheim**, the dominant reading is theological. The
  Dark Companion is the Ash-Eye — a wound in the sky left by
  something that broke or departed in a time before record.
  The Nebelheimic disposition toward it is wariness with a
  ritual edge; the world has institutions whose work is to
  watch it and to mark, in their long records, what it does
  to those who go near it.
- On **Bayurinda**, the readings are contemplative and varied.
  The Mouth of Returning is the most widely used pilgrim
  reading; the Hollow Companion is the scholarly one. The
  Bayurindan tradition holds, in some of its older texts, that
  the Dark Companion is the place where things go when they
  have finished being themselves — and that some of them, in
  the right conditions, come back. Whether the texts mean this
  literally or as a figure of teaching has been argued for
  generations.
- On **Sharazan**, the Pilgrim Star is more present in
  performance and poetry than in working astronomy. The
  Sharazani readings emphasise transformation: a thing that
  goes near it is altered, the alteration is sometimes
  desirable, and the figure of the pilgrim who returns
  changed is a recognisable shape in the world's stories.

None of these readings is doctrine. The
[[culture/knights-of-bellona|Knights of Bellona]] do not, in
public, endorse one over another. The order's own term in
its working charts is the Dark Companion, the most neutral of
the available names. What the order says internally about the
phenomenon is not part of the public record.

## Tradition and warning

There is, across all three worlds, a remembered tradition that
ancient travellers found routes that led near the Hollow
Binary, and that some of those travellers did not return, and
that some of those who returned were not entirely the same.
The tradition is older than any individual order, and it does
not survive in a single canonical form. Different worlds
preserve different fragments.

What survives is consistent enough to record. There are
warnings against _deep falling_ — against using the Dark
Companion's gravity, or its currents, or whatever it is that
it has, to attempt journeys or practices that ordinary
Nearing cannot reach. The warnings are not unanimous. Some
traditions hold that the routes still exist; some that they
should be left alone; some that they have been used in living
memory and the order is simply not saying so. The compendium
cannot resolve which of these is true.

## What it is not

The Dark Companion is not, in any reading currently endorsed,
identified with the broken god whose shattering produced
[[cosmology/skyblood|Skyblood]] and litters
[[cosmology/realms/the-void|the Void Between Worlds]]. The two
phenomena are old in different ways. Some cultural readings
gesture at a connection; none of them are scholarly. The
compendium notes the gesture and declines to confirm it.

The Dark Companion is also not, in any reading the compendium
records, alive. It does not act with intent. What it does
near pilots and Nearing happens because of what it _is_, not
because it has chosen to do anything. This is the position
the order maintains in public. Whether the order maintains it
in private is a separate question.
