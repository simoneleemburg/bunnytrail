# The Fall of Zhaor

For many cycles, all living things — creatures, races, beasts, and gods — we were alike. We walked the same roads. We told each other stories. We ate, and drank, and feasted together.

Together as one.

The world was whole. Of the world, there was only one.

What precisely occurred in those blessed days is written in no clear account. No one tells the history in the same words. In those days before, I know there was peace. And there was dance and laughter too.

There are stories, of course. Of them, I have heard many.

The dwarves speak of a wager that Holadren cast before them and the elves.

The elves recall his song — Olidammaru they name him. It was so pure that it drew tears from stone.

The Flaen, they say that he mocked the gods, without favor or fear.

And others more whisper of a love ignited between bitter tribes — rivals since the dawn of men.

Some say the seeds were sown long before. The gods — yes, the gods — they drank deep of their rising power. And the people fed them, with worship that burned brighter each day.

Whatever came to pass on that night, the world turned. And it turned forever.

Distrust took root.  
Pride festered.  
Hatred bloomed.

Zhaor, the jewel of all that was civil and grand, came under siege — a siege that bled twenty years from the world, until at last it crumbled and fell.

And in its fall, the world split. The powers had grown too vast. The weave of the universe could bear no more.

And so the gods vanished from our lands, their struggle scarred into all that remains.

Now each holds their own domain. They left us mortals in the ashes, ashes of what once was. To struggle and rebuild, forgetting what was, and to learn anew.

Thus was the world shaped.

— Rathiel, the Hermit-Priest
