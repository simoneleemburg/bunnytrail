# The Paradox of Power

Some say Pholtus and Velnius created the world. One brought the light. The other, time and flow. It is easy to believe — for who else commands such power?

Not I.

And yet… I feel that power move through me when I pray. When I heal. When I speak their names.

So I know they are real. As I know the Suel gods are real. The Baklunish gods are real. I have seen their signs. Felt their breath on the wind. Seen their wrath fall like fire from the sky.

But here is the paradox.

If the world is one, it could only have been made once. And if it was made once — by one hand, or three, or five, or seven — then why did the Suel never see Procan? Why do they speak only of Xerbo, whose name is absent from Oeridian scripture?

Why does one people feel the sea through salt and gold,  
and another through tides and omens?

Why does each remember only their gods — and not the others — when we trade and war and weep in the same world?

I have walked far and wide. I have listened to a hundred creation stories. I have read the scrolls etched in flame, and those penned in ink and fear. And in every land, I found the same powers — hidden beneath different names.

Pelor, who gives light in the East, is called Pholtus in the West. But the sun rises all the same.

Thamun breathes as Rao to some, and as all four Gods of the wind to us Oeridians. But the winds still blow.

Uuran walks as Obad-Hai, and as Velnius to some, or Lendor. But his drumbeats still sound the same.

I have come to uncover a deeper truth. One much older. The gods we name are not always the gods who were.

Some of them walked once, and rose. Others were made — of memory, of longing, of love. Or of terror.

We called out, and the divine answered —  
sometimes with fire.  
Sometimes with silence.

Not all gods made the world. But all gods are made through it. And some… by us.

This, of course, is heresy. To say that truth is not given — but scattered. Not proclaimed — but remembered. Not held — but passed down: father to son, mother to daughter, through script and song, victory and defeat, thunder and quake.

The gods are real. But what if I told you they are not as we have been told?

I must warn those who seek as I did. These are not sermons. These are my learnings — truths collected over many travels.

To read them is to risk remembering too much.

— Rathiel, the Hermit-Priest
