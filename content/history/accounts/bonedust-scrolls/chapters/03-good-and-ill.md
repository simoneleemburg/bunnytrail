# Good and Ill

It is not light that gives meaning to the sun, nor fire to its flame — but the shadow that shapes it.

I was born to Pelor. I bathed in his radiance. I served at his altar. And I have seen healing poured from his hands like water. I know warmth. I know renewal. I do not doubt him.

But I cannot ignore the silence at the edge of our chants — the name we do not say unless we curse.

Nerull.

They call him evil. Devourer. Reaper. But I have come to believe we made him that way. Not by prayer… but by fear.

He was there at the beginning. Not stalking us, but holding us. The old tribes speak of him. Neruun, they call him.

Before breath was breath, he was the bowl into which the first gods poured time and form. He did not ask for worship. He did not hunger. He waited.

And when we grew frightened of the wait — when we saw the petals fall and the breath slow — we gave that fear a name.

We called it Nerull. And then we ran from him.

The gods do not hate us. But they hear us. When enough voices whisper of monsters, even a god forgets what it was before the whisper.

Nerull was not death. He was return. He was the space we needed so there could be a next breath. He was the stillness that kept the dance from spinning wild. The stillness we needed to be born.

But we feared the stillness. And so we crowned him with bones and screamed when he approached.

I do not ask you to worship him. But perhaps, the next time you grieve, you might whisper:

> I know you were not always cruel.  
> I remember before we were afraid.

— Rathiel, the Hermit-Priest
