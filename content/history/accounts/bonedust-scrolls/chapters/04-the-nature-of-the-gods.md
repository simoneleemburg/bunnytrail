# The Nature of the Gods

Now, we begin to understand. To know what is truth from what has merely been told. We can begin to speak the true story of the gods — as the people of old, of Flanneash, still remember. As it has been told, and retold.

At first, there were the three. Those who are. Those who have always been — before space and time.

One stretched the veil.  
One lit the spark.  
One beat the drum.

Together, they created the world.

Faelor was granted the sun and the sky. The one we called Pholtus and Pelor.

Uuran was granted all that lived and changed — we now know him by his title: Obad-Hai, the Warden of the Wood.

And Neruun… Nerull, as we warped his name… he was given the realm of death and remembrance.

The world was still. Still — until it wasn't.

For there to be more, there needed to be creation.

And so, they were two. The masculine and the feminine. Thamun and Daleah — the forces that allowed all creation to be.

From them came the nine. The first to be born and inhabit the world, and all of nature. Their names: Aehlonnah, Ruun, Berhea, Thorun, Velhir, Ainya, Kaevor, Lirwen, Maelith. Mostly forgotten now, the last of the true immortals.

As the first mortals were born, so were the gods of Echoes. They are the seven. The reflections of our mortal struggle. They are mirrors. Teachers. Terrors. Thresholds.

Touching one will change you. Whether it heals or harms — that is not their burden. That is your choice to make.

One cannot say who was first, mortals or the echo gods. Who is the echo, who is the source. Eternally reverberating.

Nysirna, Holadren, Raskurn, Vaelis, Korwen, Drasha, Thyris — their names long since forgotten.

Last are the many, the hero gods. They rise. They fall. They live on in our stories. Some glorified, some feared. Many are forgotten. But once, they walked the world as we do — flawed, fierce, and fleeting.

— Rathiel, the Hermit-Priest
