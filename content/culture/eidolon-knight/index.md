An Eidolon Knight is a human who pilots an
[[eidolons|Eidolon]] — a vessel into which a
[[kinds/naya|Naya]] has been [[binding|bound]]. The
Knight is not party to the binding. The binding is between the
Naya and the vessel; the Knight is the pilot the vessel works
with, for as long as that pairing lasts.

The role is recognised on all three Sister Worlds and held by a
single interplanetary order, the
[[culture/knights-of-bellona|Knights of Bellona]]. How the role
is _received_ on each world — what a Knight is owed, how the
world around them reads what they do — varies sharply between
the worlds. What the role _is_, and where it can be acquired,
does not.

## What piloting is

Piloting an Eidolon is sustained contact between the Knight and
the Naya inside the vessel. It is, in the cosmology's own terms,
a form of [[nearing|Nearing]]: a controlled loosening
of the Knight's attachment to ordinary embodied identity, held
long enough that the Knight can move with the vessel rather
than against it. A Knight who cannot Near toward the Naya
cannot pilot the Eidolon. A Knight who Nears too far loses
themselves into the Naya and the piloting collapses.

This is the dissociative element of the work. The Knight is, in
piloting, never quite only themselves. The Naya's resonance is
present in their perception, their reflexes, their sense of
what the vessel is doing. The discipline is to stay coherent
enough to remain the pilot, while loose enough to be in working
contact with what is being piloted.

## Fit

Any Knight can, in principle, pilot any Eidolon. In practice,
fit varies. The Naya in the vessel has its own nature; the
Knight has theirs; and the two either work together or do not.
Fit is some combination of two things:

- **Piloting skill.** Training in Nearing toward Naya in
  general. Calm under prolonged dissociative pressure. The
  ability to hold one's own coherence while open. The general
  competences the order trains.
- **Compatibility with the specific Naya.** A given Naya's
  nature suits some pilots better than others. A Naya of
  storm and force responds to one kind of Knight; a Naya of
  reflection and stillness responds to another. A pilot
  working against the grain of their Naya's nature can move
  the Eidolon, but does so badly: slow response, distorted
  manifestation, the vessel doing not-quite-what was asked.

Both matter. A skilled Knight in poor fit pilots competently
but never well. A well-fitted Knight without skill struggles
to sustain the contact at all. The combination is what
produces what the order recognises as good piloting.

## The chosen Eidolon

The order's recognised ideal is the _chosen Eidolon_, sometimes
called the _chosen Naya_: a Knight who, over time, comes into
deepening working partnership with one particular Eidolon. The
fit becomes specific. The Knight learns the Naya's nature in
detail. The Naya — by whatever indications a Naya can give —
appears to accept the Knight's hand on the vessel.

This is the cultural shape Knighthood is most often imagined
in. The image is a Knight and their Eidolon, lifelong, mutually
recognisable, working together with the depth that only long
pairing produces. The order trains toward it, and ceremonial
practice celebrates it.

It is not a metaphysical requirement. A Knight without a chosen
Eidolon can still pilot. A Knight may pilot many vessels across
a career without ever settling. A Knight may take up an Eidolon
unexpectedly, in circumstances that do not allow for the slow
forming of a fit — and the piloting that results, while
recognisably piloting, is not what the chosen-Eidolon ideal
describes. The order recognises this. It does not pretend the
ideal is the only thing happening.

## What the role requires

Piloting is a sustained Nearing practice, and Nearing rewards
the same trait everywhere: **adaptive identity**. A self that
bends and recomposes under contact without fracturing. A self
stable enough to remain itself while it is, for stretches at a
time, not only itself.

Rigid identities fracture under Nearing toward a Naya. Unstable
identities dissolve into it. Knights who succeed in the role
are, almost by definition, people whose selves can take the
pressure. Whether they began that way or were made that way by
the practice is a question the order itself answers
inconsistently.

In the order's screening, this is what is being looked for. The
formal language varies. The substantive question is whether the
candidate is [[nearborn|Nearborn]] enough — adaptive
enough, already practised enough at holding a self under
resonance — to survive sustained piloting Nearing without
coming apart. The screening is imperfect. Candidates near the
margin are sometimes rejected and sometimes admitted; the order
has been wrong both ways.

[[materials/skyblood|Skyblood]] is what makes piloting possible
materially. A Knight without access to a Skyblood-cored Eidolon
is a Knight in spirit only.

## What the role costs

Sustained piloting is heavy. Even successful Nearing toward a
Naya leaves the Knight under physical and psychological strain
— the longer the use, the deeper the strain. Knights who pilot
hard, over long periods, often carry the cost visibly: fatigue
that does not fully lift, perceptions that do not fully return
to ordinary registers, a slight permanent presence of the Naya
in the way the Knight speaks and moves.

The recognised pilot failure modes mirror the broader failure
modes of Nearing:

- **Dissolving.** Nearing too far into the Naya, losing the
  thread back to one's own self. The Knight becomes less and
  less present; the piloting becomes less and less directed by
  anyone.
- **Resisting.** Refusing to Near, holding too rigidly to one's
  own embodied identity. Piloting becomes mechanical, the
  Eidolon unresponsive, the Naya disregarded. The vessel acts
  badly; sometimes it acts against the Knight.
- **Forcing.** Trying to dominate the Naya's nature rather than
  work with it. The vessel can be moved, but the manifestation
  distorts. Prolonged forcing damages the fit, sometimes
  permanently.

These are pilot failures, not binding failures. The Eidolon
itself remains bound; the Naya remains seated. What has broken
is the Knight's working contact with what they are piloting.
The order's stance is that these costs are real, that they
should be known to the Knight in advance, and that obscuring
them is a failure of duty; whether that stance is actually
maintained in practice is another question.

## The order's exclusive role

To be an Eidolon Knight is to be a Knight of Bellona. The order
trains, certifies, deploys, and oversees every known Knight in
the cosmology; it does not teach piloting outside its
membership; it does not certify Knights trained elsewhere.
Whether unsanctioned Knights exist somewhere outside the
order's view is itself a question the order's silence makes
difficult to assess. No other path to the role is publicly
known.

## How the worlds receive Knights

The order is one institution across the Sister Worlds. The
Knight standing in a Nebelheimic caldera, the Knight in
contemplative seclusion on Bayurinda, and the Knight conducting
a ceremonial duel above a Sharazani fragment are wearing the
same authority. What differs is how the world around them reads
the role.

- On [[nebelheim|Nebelheim]] the Knight is read as a
  working figure: a guarantor of defence, labour, and survival,
  whose Eidolon is for what an Eidolon can practically do. The
  Nebelheimic relationship to a Knight is closer to a duty than
  a calling — a Knight stationed on Nebelheim is something like
  an old soldier with their dependable old rifle, trusted
  because they have not failed yet and expected to keep not
  failing. The order's regulation of piloting is, on Nebelheim,
  felt as continuous with the world's own discipline.

- On [[bayurinda|Bayurinda]] the Knight is read as a
  contemplative. The cultural framing of piloting here is slow,
  ritualised, and treated as a long unfolding. A Knight may
  spend much of their tenure in study, in extended Nearing, or
  in deep ritual practice with the Naya of their chosen
  Eidolon; the Bayurindan cultural eye is patient with this,
  and treats the Knight as a figure of teaching as much as of
  action. The Eidolon may go unused in any externally visible
  sense for long stretches without the world thinking anything
  is wrong.

- On [[sharazan|Sharazan]] the Knight is read as a
  public figure. Knighthood there is invested with theatrical,
  status-bearing, and competitive meaning. Duels, exhibitions,
  and ceremonial encounters in Eidolon form are part of the
  structure of social hierarchy; Sharazani audiences expect
  their Knights to be known by name, and their Eidolons to be
  dressed for sight as much as for use. The order does not
  invent this theatre, but the order's Knights, on Sharazan,
  must operate inside it.

These are receptions, not traditions. The same Knight, moved
between worlds, finds themselves read differently. The training
that made them, and the order that authorises them, do not
change.

## History

Knighthood is the work of the Knights of Bellona, and has been
for as long as the order has had Knights to send. The "old
knight roads" of [[sharazan|Sharazan]] — ancient routes
that intersect with [[eidolons/world-pillars|World Pillar]]
paths and atmospheric travel currents — are part of the long
material record of the order's interplanetary reach. Whether
the role existed in some other form before the order took its
current shape is a question the order does not address in its
public doctrine.
