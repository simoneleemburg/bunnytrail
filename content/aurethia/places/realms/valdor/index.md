Valdor [[tha]] is the liminal realm of memory, ego, and
subjective reality in the cosmology of Alteria. It is not a
physical location in the conventional sense. Inside Valdor, time
takes on spatial qualities, and memories, narratives, emotions,
and personal associations manifest as traversable geography.
A person may move through eras the way one crosses a valley or
ascends a mountain. Distance here is measured psychologically
and narratively rather than physically.

In foundation terms, Valdor is the Aurethian name for the region
of [[mundus|Mundus]] whose [[mundus-frame|Mundus Frame]] is
rotated such that local [[equilibrium|Equilibrium]] sits near
[[self|the Self]] — what the cosmos becomes, locally, when
Subjectivity is the low point of the basin and reality bends
around whoever is doing the experiencing.

The realm appears differently to every being who enters it. Its
landscapes are shaped by ego, memory, and inner structure. One
traveller may encounter endless libraries, another flooded ruins,
another ski lifts suspended between isolated mountain peaks
emerging from clouds and fog. These forms are not illusions;
they are expressions of the traveller's own internal architecture
unfolding outward into space.

## Memory as an axis

Memory, in the Aurethian record, is treated as its own
directional axis of reality — intertwined with but distinct from
space, time, and identity. Places, objects, and civilisations
possess memory just as people do, and Valdor is the realm in
which that memory becomes navigable.

Time does not behave linearly inside Valdor. Different eras may
exist adjacent to one another spatially. Subjectivity increases
the deeper one travels; there is no fully objective experience
of Valdor, and no two accounts of it agree in their geography.

## Relationship to Nareth

Valdor and [[realms/nareth|Nareth]] are distinct but
interconnected realms. Nareth is associated with identity,
primordial truth, emotional resonance, and the dissolution of
fixed form; it is the native realm of the [[kinds/naya|Naya]].
Valdor, by contrast, concerns ego, narrative continuity, memory,
and the self as personally experienced. Where Nareth loosens
identity itself, Valdor externalises and reflects the structures
built around identity.

The two may form complementary axes of existence:

- Nareth → identity
- Valdor → memory and ego

The exact relationship between ego and identity remains
unresolved within the cosmology, and is considered an active
philosophical question rather than a settled one.

## Transformation

Within Valdor there exists a process analogous to
[[aurethia/fabric/nearing|Nearing]]. In Nareth-oriented practice, Nearing
loosens attachment to identity, bodily form, and linear
existence. In Valdor, the equivalent process manifests as
identity shift through ego and memory traversal: by moving
deeply enough through one's own narrative structures, memories,
and subjective architecture, a being may emerge transformed into
someone fundamentally different.

This transformation is never automatic. Entering Valdor does not
inherently alter identity; the shift occurs only if the traveller
willingly undergoes that passage. Because of this, Valdor is
regarded as one of the primary realms of personal transformation
within the Aurethian cosmology.

Some traditions further speculate that it may be possible to
travel from Valdor directly into Nareth without first returning
to [[realms/asthera|Asthera]], the embodied world — though
whether anyone has successfully done so is not settled in the
records.

## On the name

The word _Valdor_ is [[culture/languages/thallish|Thallish]],
like _Nareth_ and _Asthera_ before it. Its sense in the source
language clusters around thresholds, distance, and inward
passage.
