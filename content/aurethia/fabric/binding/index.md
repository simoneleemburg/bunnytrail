Binding is the pact by which a [[kinds/naya|Naya]] is seated
into an [[kinds/eidolon|Eidolon]] — bound, in the strict
sense, to the physical world by way of a vessel that can hold
it. A Naya, in itself, is a being of
[[realms/nareth|Nareth]]. It has no native foothold in
[[realms/asthera|Asthera]]. The Eidolon is what gives it
one.

This is the relationship the word _binding_ refers to: Eidolon
and Naya, vessel and being. The
[[culture/eidolon-knight|Knight]] who later pilots the
Eidolon is a third party to that pact, not a participant in it.
Knights come and go. The binding is the durable thing.

## What is bound to what

The binding is durable but specific. One Naya, one Eidolon.
A Naya bound into a particular vessel remains bound to it; the
construct becomes the shape through which that Naya is present
in the physical world. The Naya does not move between vessels.
If the Eidolon is destroyed, the Naya is not destroyed — it
returns to Nareth, or, in catastrophic cases, scatters into
[[realms/the-void|the Void Between Worlds]] — but the binding
ends with the vessel.

A bound Naya does not need a Knight to exist. It does need a
Knight to _do_ anything in the physical world at any scale
beyond its own bound form. An Eidolon with a Naya in it but no
Knight piloting it is a sleeping thing: present, real, awake in
a limited sense, but unable to move into sustained action.

## What binding requires

A Naya cannot be seated into just any construct. The Eidolon
must be built — or grown, or shaped — with the specific Naya in
view. The vessel's materials, geometry, and resonance must
accord with the Naya's nature, because the Naya is what the
vessel is _for_. A binding made into a vessel whose shape
disagrees with the Naya's nature does not stabilise; in
practice, it does not complete.

At the centre of every Eidolon is a [[materials/skyblood|Skyblood]]
core, and the core is what makes the binding possible at all.
Skyblood stabilises the Naya's presence in physical matter the
way nothing else does. Without it the seating cannot be sustained;
with it, the Naya can remain.

What binding produces is durable in proportion to what went
into it: an Eidolon built with care, in honest contact with the
Naya's nature, holds. An Eidolon built carelessly or against
its Naya's nature does not, and the failure modes are recognised
across all three Sister Worlds.

## Failure states

When binding goes wrong, it goes wrong in the vessel:

- **Unstable seating**, where the Naya's presence in the Eidolon
  fluctuates — the construct becomes intermittently inert, or
  the Naya's manifestation distorts under load.
- **Distorted Eidolon forms**, where the construct's
  manifestation no longer matches the Naya it carries. The
  vessel becomes the wrong shape for what is inside it.
- **Loss of binding**, where the Naya is released back to Nareth
  before the Eidolon is destroyed. The construct remains; what
  was inside it does not.

These failures originate in the binding, not in the Knight.
A Knight piloting an unstably bound Eidolon will struggle in
ways that look like piloting failure, but the underlying
problem is in the vessel.

## What the Knight is, in this picture

The Knight is to the Eidolon roughly what a rider is to a
horse. The horse exists with or without the rider. The rider
gives the horse direction, purpose, occasion. A good pairing
makes both of them more than either alone; a poor pairing
leaves both diminished. The horse has its own nature, which
the rider can work with or against. The horse is not bound to
the rider.

This is, very approximately, the relationship. Piloting
itself is a sustained practice of contact between Knight and
the Naya inside the vessel — a form of
[[nearing|Nearing]] toward the Naya through the
Eidolon. It is its own discipline, treated in the
[[culture/eidolon-knight|Eidolon Knight]] entry. What matters
here is that the binding sits underneath all of that. The
Knight is not party to it.

## The core ethical question

The cosmology has never quite settled whether Naya are
partners in their binding or objects of it. Different worlds
answer differently. Different generations of the same world
answer differently. A Naya bound into a well-built Eidolon
appears, by every indication those in sustained contact can
read, to consent to that seating — but the indications are
inferential, the Naya does not speak in any human sense, and
the order's confidence on the point varies between centuries.
The question is one of the central ethical conflicts of
Alteria, and it is unlikely ever to be fully resolved.
