Humans are a prominent embodied people of Alteria — mortal,
materially bound, and broadly recognisable as the kind of creature
that walks, builds, remembers, and dies. They are native to
[[realms/asthera|Asthera]], the material plane.

What makes them unusual in Alteria's cosmology is less their bodies
than what their bodies hold. Humans sustain persistent, high-intensity
emotional experience — grief that lasts years, devotion that survives
distance, the slow accumulations of love and resentment and hope — in
a way that few other beings can. To embodied life this is simply how
existence feels. To the [[beings/naya|Naya]], looking in from
[[realms/nareth|Nareth]], it is something rarer: a stability of
feeling, and a vividness of feeling, that they cannot generate on
their own.

## Why the Naya come to them

The Naya are drawn to humans for exactly this reason. Most of
the cosmology's sustained Naya–human contact runs through
[[binding|binding]] — but binding, in the strict
sense, is the pact between a Naya and an
[[eidolons|Eidolon]], not between a Naya and a
person. What humans offer the Naya is something the bound Naya
reaches for _through_ its vessel and the
[[culture/eidolon-knight|Knight]] piloting it: sustained
contact with a kind of emotional life Naya cannot generate on
their own substrate.

The Knight, in piloting, opens that contact. The Naya, in
being piloted, lives through it. The arrangement is not a
gift in either direction. It is a relation; everyone involved
is changed.

## Mortality, memory, and the cost

Humans die. Asthera is the plane of persistence, but its persistence
is the persistence of form, not of any particular form forever; bodies
hold for a lifetime and then return their material to the world.
This is the central asymmetry of human–Naya relations. Naya, native
to a layer where identity drifts but does not end, must come to terms
with a partner whose entire participation has a horizon. Humans, for
their part, must come to terms with a partner who will outlast them
and may not understand, at first, what that means.

Mortality is therefore not a flaw in humans, from the cosmological
point of view. It is part of what they offer: a life with a shape,
with stakes, with an ending that gives the feelings their weight.
