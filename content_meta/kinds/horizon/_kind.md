A horizon is not a place. It is a limit — a direction along
which things can lean, and a condition things can approach,
but a state massive existence cannot reach.

Three horizons bound the interior of existence: the Source, the
Self, and Chaos. They are not opposed to one another and they
are not opposed to Mundus. They are the geometry within which
Mundus is shaped.

## The mass condition

Approaching a horizon is asymptotic to mass. The closer a thing
leans toward a horizon, the more its mass resists — the same
shape as the relativistic limit on Earth, where reaching the
speed of light is asymptotic to mass and forbidden to anything
with rest mass.

A thing with mass cannot be at a horizon. It can only approach
one, from inside Mundus, by however much its nature permits.

Whether anything _without_ mass is native to the horizons — and
what such a thing would be — is left open. The foundation layer
does not enumerate it; different clusters' accounts vary in
what they name and how.

## Local accounts

Particular clusters give the horizons their own names and
describe the regions of Mundus that lean closest to each.
Aurethia knows the region of Mundus closest to the Source as
[[realms/nareth|Nareth]]; the region closest to the Self as
[[realms/valdor|Valdor]]; the region of Mundus that leans
hardest toward Chaos as [[realms/the-void|the Void Between
Worlds]]. None of these are the horizons themselves — they are
the parts of Mundus nearest to them.
